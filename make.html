what it can build from

The pieces

The model picks from these and fills them in. It cannot write code or markup, which is why an app made from a sentence is safe to open.

prose
A sentence or two in your own voice: how to use this app, or what to do first. Connective tissue, not the content.
note
A short aside. Use `warn` to say something the brief asked for that this app cannot do, or that you had to guess at.
checklist
Tick-boxes that stay ticked on the device. Use for a study routine, a packing list, steps to do in order, or anything that gets done and checked off.
flashcards
Question on the front, answer on the back, tap to flip. The right block whenever the brief involves studying, memorising, quizzing, vocabulary, dates, formulas or facts. Write real cards from what the brief says; if the brief gives no material, write starter cards about how to study the subject and say so in a note.
prompts
Open questions to ask out loud — for a parent quizzing a child, a practice interview, or rehearsing an explanation. Use when the point is talking it through rather than recalling a fact.
timer
A countdown with a start button. Use for focused study blocks, timed practice, or a break. Between 1 and 90 minutes.
photos
Slots for pictures the person will take or upload: the textbook page, the worksheet, the whiteboard. Include this whenever the brief mentions pictures, photos, uploads, pages or notes to capture. Pictures stay on the device; say what to photograph in `note`.
